Loaiza and Rigobón (MIT Sloan, 2025) introduce the EPOCH framework — five human capability groups (Empathy, Presence/Networking, Opinion/Judgment/Ethics, Creativity, Hope/Vision/Leadership) — and three quantitative metrics (EPOCH index, risk-of-substitution score, augmentation score) computed via network-based methods mapping task interdependencies across 947 US occupations in O*NET. Regression analysis of 2016 and 2024 O*NET data finds new tasks emerging in 2024 score 0.256 standard deviations higher on the EPOCH scale than retired tasks (p<0.01), and high-EPOCH tasks increased in frequency between 2016 and 2024. At the occupational level, higher EPOCH scores predicted stronger employment growth from 2015–2023 (β=0.132 per SD, ~12,000 additional jobs), higher 2025 hiring rates, and favorable BLS projections through 2034 (β=0.114, p<0.01); high-automation-risk occupations showed consistent employment declines across all three horizons (β=−0.219 historically, ~20,000 fewer jobs). Augmentation scores were associated with employment decline in the past decade but showed no statistically significant link to long-run projections, indicating that AI-driven productivity enhancement does not automatically translate into employment growth for augmented roles.
