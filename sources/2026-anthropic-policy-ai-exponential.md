Anthropic's "Policy on the AI Exponential" blog post announces two companion policy proposals — the Advanced AI Framework (catastrophic-risk governance for frontier developers) and an Economic Policy Framework addressing AI's broader economic impacts. The post cites Claude Mythos Preview's discovery of thousands of high-severity vulnerabilities across every major operating system and browser as evidence that AI capability acceleration now requires government enforcement authority rather than transparency requirements alone. It summarizes the Advanced AI Framework's developer obligation requirements (testing, transparency, independent evaluation, security, enforcement authority with civil penalties) and its societal resilience recommendations for biological and cyber threats, and outlines a US preemption position: Congress should not preempt state AI law unless it enacts a federal regime at least as strong as the proposed framework, with even then preemption narrowly scoped to specific frontier governance functions and not reading to displace state authority over AI regulation generally.
