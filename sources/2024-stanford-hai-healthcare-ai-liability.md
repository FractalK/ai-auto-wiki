This Stanford HAI news article summarizes a 2024 New England Journal of Medicine piece by Stanford Law and Medicine professor Michelle Mello and JD/PhD student Neel Guha on liability risk from healthcare AI tools. Two core challenges are identified: the absence of an independent regulatory testing process analogous to FDA drug approval (companies currently self-test), and courts filling the resulting vacuum without established legal frameworks for AI causation in patient harm cases. A counterintuitive liability dynamic is described — opaque, poorly performing models may be harder to successfully litigate against than transparent, high-performing ones because attributing causation requires demonstrating that a comprehensible output caused the harm. Mello offers four hospital risk management recommendations: tiered monitoring intensity proportional to tool risk, meticulous documentation of model version and software details, contractual negotiation to shift liability to AI developers, and disclosure of AI tool use to patients.
