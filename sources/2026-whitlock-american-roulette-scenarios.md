Chris Whitlock, an AI integration specialist and former Deloitte advanced analytics leader, introduces a four-scenario framework for analyzing AI's economic and social trajectory in the United States over the next decade. The framework organizes AI futures by two binary uncertainties — whether superintelligence is achievable, and whether it can be controlled — producing four scenarios: Doldrums (AI stalls and investment collapses without delivering promised returns), Workforce Wipeout (AI automation expands significantly but falls short of superintelligence, potentially driving unemployment to 20% or higher), Singularity (superintelligence achieved and controlled, enabling perpetual productivity growth and post-scarcity abundance), and Cataclysm (superintelligence achievable but uncontrollable). Whitlock argues that practitioners and business leaders should plan for a sequential traversal of multiple scenarios rather than betting on a single outcome, and highlights structural vulnerabilities in the US economy — a consumption-based GDP model reliant on labor income, and a federal revenue system drawing approximately 84% of receipts from income and payroll taxes — that make the transition period between current AI and potential superintelligence structurally risky regardless of which scenario ultimately prevails.
