The University of Bristol Library has adapted the CRAAP evaluation model — originally developed by librarian Sarah Blakeslee at California State University Chico for general information source evaluation — to guide structured assessment of AI outputs and AI tools across five criteria: Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, and Purpose. Each criterion identifies a category of risk specific to AI: Currency flags that even current-dataset tools may produce outdated references; Relevance notes that AI cannot generate comprehensive, replicable systematic literature review search strategies since paywalled scholarly content is excluded and AI-preselected search logic cannot be audited or replicated; Authority highlights that AI outputs underrepresent non-Western and non-English-language perspectives due to training data skew, and authorship or expertise cannot be attributed to AI-generated content; Accuracy addresses hallucination and fabricated citations requiring independent verification of every claim; and Purpose covers commercial opacity, data privacy, equity concerns, and academic integrity implications of AI tool use. A downloadable PDF checklist accompanies the online guide for practical classroom and self-study use.
