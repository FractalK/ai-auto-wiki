Anthropic's Advanced AI Framework proposes a two-part federal regulatory structure for frontier AI. Developer obligations apply to companies training models above 10²⁵ FLOPs with more than \$500M in AI-derived revenue or more than \$1B in annual AI R&D spending, requiring mandatory testing for four enumerated catastrophic risk categories — biological weapons, offensive cyber operations, loss of control, and automated R&D — along with published safety frameworks, system cards, biannual risk reports, engagement of independent evaluators, security programs protecting model weights and training infrastructure, and government authority empowered to block or deter deployment of models posing significant catastrophic risk, with civil penalties scaling with global revenue. The societal resilience portion recommends layered defenses for biological threats (prevention through modernized biosafety standards, gene synthesis screening, and threat intelligence sharing; detection through pathogen-agnostic biosurveillance and microbial forensics; preparedness and response through stockpiling, AI-accelerated countermeasure development, and binding after-action reviews) and for cyber threats (hardening open-source and legacy software, forward-deployed support for under-resourced critical infrastructure operators, government tracking of frontier cyber capabilities, and coordinated vulnerability remediation at scale). The framework explicitly notes that the societal resilience agenda for loss-of-control and automated R&D risks is substantially less developed than biological and cyber resilience and requires much more active work across governments, researchers, and industry.
