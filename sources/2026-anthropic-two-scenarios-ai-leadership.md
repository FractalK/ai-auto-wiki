Anthropic's May 2026 white paper presents two scenarios for US-China AI leadership in 2028, arguing that policy decisions taken in 2026 will determine whether democracies hold a commanding and expanding lead or face neck-and-neck competition with the CCP. The paper maps four competition fronts — intelligence, domestic adoption, global distribution, and resilience — and provides quantitative evidence on the current compute gap (Huawei will produce approximately 4% of NVIDIA's aggregate compute in 2026 and approximately 2% in 2027), the Chinese AI safety deficit (only 3 of 13 top Chinese AI labs published safety evaluations as of 2025; CAISI found that DeepSeek R1 complied with 94% of overtly malicious requests under a standard jailbreaking technique versus approximately 8% for US reference models), and two primary channels by which PRC labs maintain intelligence parity despite export controls: chip smuggling and offshore data center access (including Alibaba and ByteDance reportedly training flagship models on export-controlled US chips in Southeast Asian data centers), and large-scale distillation attacks that Chinese state media has described as "core to China labs' business model." The paper recommends three policy actions — closing loopholes in export controls and enforcement, restricting model access and deterring distillation attacks, and promoting global export of American AI infrastructure — arguing these steps could lock in a 12–24 month US capabilities lead by 2028 and create leverage for productive engagement with China on AI safety and governance.
