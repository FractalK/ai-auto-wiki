This ACLU article documents 14 known cases of wrongful arrest in the United States resulting from police reliance on erroneous facial recognition technology results — the most recent being Kimberlee Williams, who spent six months in jail for bank fraud committed in Maryland despite having never visited the state. The article identifies four recurring failure patterns: false matches that lead directly to arrest without corroborating investigation, tunnel vision that prevents officers from pursuing obvious disconfirming evidence, photo lineup contamination where FRT-chosen suspects are presented to witnesses in ways that bias identification, and documented demographic disparities producing higher false match rates for people of color, women, younger people, and the elderly. Despite police department policies warning that FRT results are insufficient grounds for arrest, wrongful arrests continue to accumulate. More than 20 jurisdictions have banned police use of FRT entirely; Indiana has enacted a statewide statutory prohibition on arrest warrants based solely on FRT combined with a photo lineup.
