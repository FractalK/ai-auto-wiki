The Munich Regional Court issued a preliminary ruling in June 2026 finding Google liable for false statements generated by its AI Overviews search feature. The case arose after two publishers discovered that Google's AI-generated summaries linked them — without factual basis — to questionable business practices and subscription-related frauds, by combining information about other flagged companies with data from the plaintiffs. The court held that AI Overviews produced "independent, new, and substantial statements" rather than merely displaying third-party content, eliminating the safe-harbor defense available to link-based search engines and making Google the author of record for the false associations. User-facing disclaimers acknowledging AI error potential were found insufficient to exempt Google from liability, on the grounds that victims of AI-fabricated associations have no third party to hold accountable since the false claims originated in the AI's synthesis rather than in any source document. The ruling is a preliminary decision subject to appeal and has potential implications for other AI search and summarization systems whose outputs synthesize new claims from aggregated source material.
