Based on 52 structured interviews, 153 senior leader surveys, and systematic review of 300+ public AI initiatives conducted January–June 2025, this preliminary MIT NANDA report documents a stark adoption-outcome split the authors term the "GenAI Divide": 95% of organizations show zero measurable P&L return from GenAI investment, while 5% achieve multi-million-dollar value extraction through learning-capable, workflow-embedded systems. Only two of eight major industry sectors — Technology and Media & Telecom — show meaningful structural disruption from AI; seven sectors exhibit high pilot activity but no material change to business models, market structure, or workflow fundamentals. The primary barrier to scale is identified as a learning gap — AI tools that cannot retain feedback, adapt to specific workflows, or improve over time — rather than model quality, regulation, or talent access. Organizational design findings show external partnership approaches achieving successful deployment approximately twice as often as internal builds (~67% vs. ~33%), and back-office automation (BPO elimination, agency spend reduction) consistently delivering higher ROI than front-office sales and marketing functions that receive approximately 70% of AI budgets. The report carries significant methodological limitations: sample bias toward organizations willing to discuss AI challenges, self-reported success metrics, and a six-month observation window that may undercount long-term implementation success.
