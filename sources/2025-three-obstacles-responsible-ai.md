Based on interviews with over 20 AI leaders, ethics officers, and senior executives across technology, financial services, healthcare, and the public sector, Isık and Goswami identify three structural gaps that prevent organizations from operationalizing responsible AI principles: an Accountability Gap (diffuse ownership with no defined project-level accountability for responsible AI), a Strategy Gap (ethics positioned in compliance functions consulted after product and business decisions are made), and a Resource Gap (systematic underinvestment in trained reviewers, monitoring bandwidth, and calibrated evaluation tools). The authors propose the SHARP framework — Structure ownership at the project level, Hardwire ethics into everyday procedures, Align ethical risk with business risk, Reward responsible behavior, Practice ethical judgment — as five organizational strategies to convert responsible AI aspirations into sustainable operational practice, emphasizing that the primary barrier to implementation is organizational, not technical.
