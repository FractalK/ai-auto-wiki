This UNU-INWEH institutional report examines AI's environmental footprint through the combined lens of carbon, water, and land costs — moving beyond the carbon-only framing that dominates most AI sustainability discourse. It establishes that inference, not training, accounts for an estimated 80–90% of total AI energy consumption; that task energy intensity varies by five orders of magnitude from text classification to high-resolution video generation; and that 2025 global data center electricity consumption reached 448 TWh, projected to exceed 945 TWh by 2030. The report documents the Jevons Paradox as a structural challenge to efficiency-only approaches — efficiency improvements lower per-use cost, driving volume increases that may erase total resource savings — and proposes six guiding principles for a responsible AI ecosystem: transparency, efficiency by design, equity and environmental justice, lifecycle responsibility, global cooperation, and sustainable use.
