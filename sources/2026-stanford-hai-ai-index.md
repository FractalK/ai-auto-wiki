The AI Index 2026 Annual Report is Stanford HAI's ninth edition comprehensively tracking AI development across research and development, technical performance, responsible AI, economy, science, medicine, education, policy and governance, and public opinion. Its central finding is that AI capability is accelerating on multiple dimensions — frontier models now match or exceed human-level performance on PhD-level science, mathematical olympiad problems, and autonomous software engineering benchmarks — while governance frameworks, evaluation methods, and institutional infrastructure are not keeping pace. Key documented patterns include: benchmark saturation compressing evaluation timelines; responsible AI benchmarking lagging capability benchmarking; documented AI incidents rising 55% year-over-year to 362; AI sovereignty emerging as a central organizing principle of national AI policy across five dimensions (infrastructure, data, model, application, talent); US state AI bills surging from fewer than 10 in 2020 to 150 in 2025; and a systematic 50-point expert-public gap on AI's job impact, with the United States reporting the lowest trust in its own government to regulate AI (31%) of any surveyed country.
