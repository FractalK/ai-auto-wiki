A 2025 joint MIT Sloan Management Review and Boston Consulting Group expert panel of international AI practitioners and academics, supplemented by a survey of 1,221 executives, finds that 69% believe agentic AI requires fundamentally new management approaches, while a 25% minority argues that existing delegation frameworks can be adapted. The panel identifies five organizational recommendations: adopt lifecycle-based iterative management, integrate explicit human accountability structures into AI governance, enable AI-led decisions in defined circumstances while preserving human oversight for higher-risk scenarios, prepare for AI systems that autonomously create or modify other AI systems ("AI offspring"), and make implicit governance structures explicit. A core finding is that agentic AI systems require explicitly defined rules, threshold values, permissible decision scopes, data usage guardrails, ethical boundaries, and escalation confidence thresholds that human workers navigate through implicit judgment — creating governance requirements outside traditional management models. Accountability for agentic AI cannot rest with the AI system itself given its lack of legal personhood; it must be distributed across creators, deployers, and users throughout the AI lifecycle.
