Hitzig et al. analyze approximately 400,000 privacy-preserved Claude Code sessions from approximately 235,000 users between October 2025 and April 2026, finding persistent returns to domain expertise across all success measures and a stable natural division of labor in which users make approximately 70% of planning decisions while Claude makes approximately 80% of execution decisions. Expert-rated sessions generate more than twice the Claude actions per prompt (12 versus 5 for novice users) and five times the output volume, while every major occupation succeeds in code-producing sessions at rates within 7 percentage points of software engineers — establishing domain expertise, not coding background, as the primary predictor of performance. Over the study window, session composition shifted toward higher-value end-to-end agentic work as debugging fell from 33% to 19% of sessions and operating software grew from 14% to 21%, and the estimated value of the average session rose approximately 27%. Verified success rates were 15% for novice-rated sessions versus 28–33% for intermediate through expert sessions, with novice users abandoning troubled sessions (failed with zero code written) at 19% versus 5–7% for others — identifying domain expertise as both a quality amplifier and a failure recovery mechanism in agentic coding.
