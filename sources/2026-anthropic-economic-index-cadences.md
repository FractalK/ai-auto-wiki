The June 2026 Anthropic Economic Index report extends the Economic Index primitives framework with hourly-resolution telemetry, artifact classification for chat and Cowork conversations, and the first Anthropic Economic Index Survey. Chapter 2's artifact analysis finds 93% of chat and Cowork conversations produce a concrete output, with token consumption scaling with the wage value of mapped tasks (2.07x in top-wage-tercile conversations) while user turns and Claude output per turn rise in tandem — a pattern the authors characterize as labor-augmenting rather than displacing. Chapter 3 links survey responses from approximately 9,700 Claude users to their usage data, finding anticipated AI capability growth is uniform across occupational exposure levels and that users with higher automation share are more optimistic about AI's impact on their job outcomes across all six measured dimensions. The survey sample skews heavily toward computer/math (30%) and management (23%) occupations and is not representative of the general workforce; Anthropic's institutional interest in favorable characterizations of AI's economic role should be noted when interpreting findings about labor augmentation and worker optimism.
