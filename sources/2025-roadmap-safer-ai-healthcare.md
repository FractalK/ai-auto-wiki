A Stanford News article summarizing a JAMA report co-authored by Mello, Hernandez-Boussard, and Shah recommends four priorities for responsible AI integration in health care: multistakeholder engagement throughout an AI tool's life cycle, robust real-world evaluation tools and methods, a national data infrastructure for shared learning across health systems (modeled on the FDA's Sentinel initiative), and stronger regulatory frameworks including an expanded FDA oversight role. The authors argue that AI adoption in clinical settings has substantially outpaced governance structures, noting that most health systems lack the in-house infrastructure and resources to evaluate clinical AI rigorously, that direct-to-consumer health apps marketed as low-risk wellness tools largely evade FDA regulatory scrutiny, and that even FDA-cleared clinical AI tools are often not required to demonstrate real-world patient outcome improvements beyond technical performance metrics.
