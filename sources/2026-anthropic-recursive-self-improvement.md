The Anthropic Institute's June 2026 post documents AI-accelerated AI development through internal Anthropic data and external benchmarks, arguing that the trend toward recursive self-improvement is already underway and may produce a system capable of fully autonomously designing its own successor sooner than institutions are prepared for. As of May 2026, more than 80% of Anthropic's merged production codebase is authored by Claude, engineers are merging 8x as much code per day as in 2024, and Claude's session success rate on open-ended engineering tasks reached 76% — up 50 percentage points in six months. METR's external task horizon benchmark shows AI autonomous task completion doubling every four months, from approximately 4 minutes (Claude Opus 3, March 2024) to 12 hours (Claude Opus 4.6, approximately March 2026) to at least 16 hours (Claude Mythos Preview, May 2026). The post proposes that the world should have the option to slow or temporarily pause frontier AI development, contingent on building multi-party verification infrastructure analogous to nuclear arms control, while acknowledging that competitive incentives make defection from any coordinated slowdown highly likely without such infrastructure.
