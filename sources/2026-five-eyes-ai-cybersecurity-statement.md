A joint statement from the heads of six Five Eyes cybersecurity agencies — ASD (Australia), CSE (Canada), GCSB (New Zealand), NCSC (UK via GCHQ), NSA, and CISA (US) — characterizes AI as "rapidly transforming cyber risk" and calls on organizational leaders to treat cybersecurity as a core board-level business risk rather than a purely technical issue. The statement asserts that frontier AI will "fundamentally transform both offensive and defensive cyber capabilities" on a timeline of months rather than years, and identifies four organizational action areas as now urgent: reducing attack surfaces, accelerating patching given AI's compression of the vulnerability-to-exploitation window, addressing legacy systems as strategic liabilities, and preparing for incidents proactively through testing and team training. The agencies call on organizations to integrate AI into their security operations to detect vulnerabilities earlier and respond faster, while acknowledging that AI simultaneously lowers the barrier for malicious actors and increases attack speed and complexity. The statement is notable as the first coordinated declaration at head-of-agency level from all five allied nations' cybersecurity and intelligence organizations specifically addressing AI-accelerated cyber risk to private-sector organizational leaders.
