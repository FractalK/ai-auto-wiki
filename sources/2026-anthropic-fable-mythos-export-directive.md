Anthropic's official public statement responding to the US government's export control directive to suspend access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5. The statement discloses that the directive was received at 5:21pm ET on 2026-06-12, cited national security authority, and provided no specific details of the security concern. Anthropic characterizes the government's finding as a non-universal jailbreak — specifically, asking the model to read a specific codebase and identify software flaws — and presents its own assessment that the demonstrated capability is available in other publicly deployed models including GPT-5.5 and is used routinely in defensive security contexts. The statement describes Anthropic's defense-in-depth strategy for Fable 5: accepting that non-universal jailbreaks are inherent to all current AI systems, while aiming to make them narrow and expensive, combined with monitoring via mandated 30-day data retention. Anthropic states it is complying with the directive while publicly disputing its proportionality, arguing the action fails to adhere to the company's stated principles for legitimate government AI oversight — a transparent, fair, statutory process grounded in technical facts. Source is vendor-authored and reflects Anthropic's institutional perspective; claims about comparisons to other models and the nature of the jailbreak should be read with awareness of this posture.
