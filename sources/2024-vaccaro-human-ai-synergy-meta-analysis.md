Vaccaro, Almaatouq, and Malone conduct a preregistered systematic review and meta-analysis of 106 experimental studies (370 effect sizes, January 2020–June 2023) to identify when human-AI combinations outperform either humans or AI alone. The central finding is that human-AI combinations achieve augmentation on average — outperforming humans alone (Hedges' g = 0.64) — but not synergy: they perform significantly worse than the best of humans or AI alone (g = −0.23), meaning it would typically have been more effective to use either partner alone. Two moderators are statistically significant: task type (decision tasks show losses; creation tasks trend toward gains) and relative baseline performance (synergy occurs when humans outperform AI alone; large losses occur when AI outperforms humans). Neither AI explanations nor confidence scores improve human-AI performance, challenging a dominant assumption in the explainable AI literature. The paper argues that process design — explicitly assigning different subtasks to whichever partner performs better — is the most promising but least-studied lever for achieving true synergy.
