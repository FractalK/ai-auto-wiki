Rigobon and Loaiza-Saa (MIT Sloan) present the EPOCH framework — identifying five human capability groups (Empathy, Presence/Networking, Opinion/Judgment/Ethics, Creativity, Hope/Vision/Leadership) as the task domains where human-AI complementarity is strongest because AI consistently underperforms in each group. The research analyzes O*NET labor data spanning 2016–2024 and finds that tasks newly added to O*NET in 2024 exhibit higher EPOCH capability levels than pre-existing or removed tasks, consistent with a labor market shifting toward EPOCH-intensive work as AI adoption expands. The paper also identifies four statistical boundary conditions under which AI tools fail regardless of task domain — biased training data, small sample sizes, extrapolation beyond training range, and moral dilemma resolution — defining the structural zones where human judgment remains essential. This press release summarizes the underlying research paper "The EPOCH of AI: Human-Machine Complementarities at Work"; the research paper itself was not ingested directly.
