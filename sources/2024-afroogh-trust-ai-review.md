A systematic literature review of 329 papers on trust in AI, presenting a taxonomy of ten trustworthiness metrics — seven non-technical (explainability, transparency/interpretability, empathy, privacy, fairness, accountability, and XAI frameworks) and three technical (safety, accuracy, and robustness) — and analyzing the major causes of distrust. The authors propose a fundamental distinction between trust (a user's relational disposition toward an AI system) and trustworthiness (an intrinsic property of the system), finding these are entirely disentangled: a trustworthy AI may not be trusted, and users may trust an untrustworthy system. Distrust is taxonomized into three structural classes — surveillance and manipulation threats, threats to human autonomy and dignity, and concerns about unpredictable AI futures — each requiring different mitigation strategies. The paper introduces the "trust equity problem," the finding that trust in AI is distributed unevenly across demographic groups in ways that may exacerbate existing workplace inequalities, and identifies interpersonal trust as structurally impossible in human-AI relationships because AI systems lack the intentionality and benevolence that ground human-to-human trust.
