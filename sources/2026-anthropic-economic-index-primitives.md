The fourth Anthropic Economic Index report (January 2026) introduces five foundational measurement dimensions — economic "primitives" — derived from privacy-preserving analysis of 1 million Claude.ai conversations and 1 million first-party API records from November 2025, prior to the release of Opus 4.5. The five primitives cover task complexity, human and AI skill levels, use case (work/coursework/personal), AI autonomy, and task success rate, each validated as directionally accurate against external benchmarks and human ratings. Using these primitives, the report documents real-world task horizons (50% task success rate at 3.5 hours of human-equivalent task duration for API users; approximately 19 hours for Claude.ai multi-turn sessions), revises effective AI job coverage to 49% of jobs at a 25% task-penetration threshold by weighting task coverage with success rates and time allocation, and adjusts aggregate productivity estimates from 1.8 to 1.0–1.2 percentage points per year once task reliability is incorporated. The analysis additionally finds that Claude disproportionately covers tasks requiring higher formal education than the economy-wide average (mean 14.4 vs. 13.2 years), producing a net deskilling effect across most occupations when covered tasks are removed. Geographic analysis shows strong GDP-per-capita correlation with adoption and estimates US state-level usage converging to parity within 2–5 years — approximately 10x faster than prior economically consequential technology diffusions.
