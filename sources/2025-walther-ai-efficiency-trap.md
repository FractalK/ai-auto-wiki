This article argues that AI productivity tools create a four-stage efficiency trap — initial gains, managerial recalibration upward, dependency acceleration, and performance expectation lock-in — that progressively erodes worker agency through "agency decay." Walther introduces the concept of learned technological helplessness, a state in which workers become psychologically dependent on AI for tasks they previously performed independently, and identifies a parallel collective action problem at industry level in which individually rational AI utilization decisions produce collectively irrational outcomes. The POZE framework (Perspective, Optimization, Zeniths, Exposure) is proposed as an organizational tool for setting explicit caps on AI-driven expectation escalation and preserving human autonomous judgment. The source is a contributed practitioner piece by a visiting Wharton scholar with a humanitarian background; it synthesizes third-party empirical studies rather than conducting original research.
