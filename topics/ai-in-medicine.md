AI tools designed to automate clinical documentation — particularly ambient scribes that generate clinical notes from patient visits — saw widespread adoption across hospital systems in 2025. Reports from multiple health systems document physicians spending up to 83% less time on clinical note-writing, with significant reductions in physician burnout. This class of tool represents one of the more clearly documented examples of AI delivering measurable workflow benefits in a high-stakes professional environment.

However, the evidence base supporting clinical AI more broadly remains thin. A review of more than 500 published clinical AI studies found that nearly half relied on exam-style questions rather than real patient data, and only 5% used actual clinical data. This evaluative gap means that most published AI-in-medicine research reflects simulated performance rather than real-world clinical utility, limiting the generalizability of published findings to actual patient care contexts.
