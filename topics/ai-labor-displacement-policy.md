AI's potential to displace workers at scale is the central concern in Anthropic's June 2026 Economic Policy Framework. In a June 2026 public interview, Dario Amodei reaffirmed that he remains at the same order of concern as his prior estimate that AI could eliminate approximately 50% of all entry-level white collar jobs within one to five years — a figure he had declined to revise upward despite a year of rapid capability gains. He simultaneously proposed UBI and progressive taxation of AI companies as Tier 3-level mitigation measures if disruption reaches transformative scale. The document opens with a values statement: Anthropic is not seeking job displacement, considers some displacement an intrinsic consequence of the technology it is building, and frames its responsibility as preparing for and responding to that displacement — not eliminating it. This positions the framework as a preparation document rather than a capabilities constraint. Early-stage evidence already shows entry-level workers in AI-exposed occupations facing weaker employment growth; economists' models range from modest productivity-driven growth to scenarios where output doubles but wages collapse.

The framework is organized around three tiers calibrated to unemployment severity, paired with three foundational preconditions that must be built before any tier's interventions can be deployed at scale. Each tier builds on the prior. Anthropic explicitly flags that some Tier 1 and Tier 2 tools appropriate for transitory disruption may entrench the link between income and employment in ways that make Tier 3 harder to navigate — and notes these tools may need to be phased out if conditions move toward Tier 3.

## The Three-Tier Framework

**Tier 1: Baseline economy (~5% unemployment with churn)** targets the period when headline unemployment appears normal but AI is steadily compressing the value of acquired skills. Primary interventions: universal pre-distributive capital accounts (seeded at birth, expanded to adults in AI-exposed occupations, with flexible withdrawal for retraining and relocation); wage insurance for workers who accept lower-paying jobs after displacement; occupational licensing reform to ease re-entry into licensed occupations; retention tax incentives for firms that redeploy rather than eliminate workers; and workforce training grants for employer-connected sectoral programs. Pre-distributive accounts are explicitly framed as long-horizon: they must start compounding before disruption is visible to matter when it arrives.

**Tier 2: Recession-level disruption (~10% unemployment)** activates when disruption is a fact of life for tens of millions of workers, with job searches stretching from months into years and familiar career pathways becoming impassable. The central intervention is expanded unemployment insurance, with automatic and uniform state-level triggers replacing the current patchy Extended Benefits system. Additional measures include sector-specific transition support (extended income support, subsidized retraining, relocation assistance) and basic needs relief for workers who exhaust UI or who never qualified for traditional UI.

**Tier 3: Transformative disruption (unemployment beyond historical peaks)** addresses the scenario where the economy is past the edge of existing policy maps — record aggregate output alongside sustained unemployment, with the historical link between contributing to the economy and sharing in its rewards strained or broken. At this tier, the framework proposes expanding existing income support toward a common floor, and identifies candidate mechanisms for new revenue and redistribution. The framework explicitly states Anthropic is not yet ready to advocate specific Tier 3 policies but commits to investing in research to evaluate them.

## Foundation Requirements

**Measurement.** Current government statistical infrastructure was built for slower-moving economies and does not track AI adoption or its labor-market effects with the speed or granularity this moment requires. Expanded investment in statistical agencies, AI-specific survey instruments, and reporting requirements for AI labs and firms — including Anthropic — is needed. Instruments should focus on directly observable metrics: AI usage rates, intensity of use, and worker productivity by adoption level.

**A government tracking unit.** A dedicated unit to monitor how AI is moving through the economy sector by sector, stress-test institutions that will have to absorb disruption, and flag early signals for policy response — analogous to the Council of Economic Advisers created for the post-war economic transition.

**Delivery infrastructure.** The US unemployment insurance system is unprepared for a large labor market shock: many states run legacy systems that buckled under pandemic claim volumes. Modernized systems should process claims faster, flex automatically when Congress or states adjust benefits, and run real-time identity and fraud matching. Only 52% of the world's population is covered by any social protection scheme; countries should invest now in the systems that would need to expand rapidly under disruption.

## Teaching Notes

**Concept in plain terms.** AI labor displacement policy refers to the government interventions being designed to manage economic disruption if AI replaces significant numbers of workers. Anthropic's June 2026 framework is the first detailed proposal from a frontier AI company — organized not as abstract principles but as concrete interventions calibrated to how severe the disruption actually turns out to be, with clear triggers (unemployment rates) for escalating from one tier to the next.

**Why it matters for instruction.** This framework matters for practitioners because it reveals what a leading AI developer itself anticipates about the disruptive potential of its technology. It also models a specific preparedness logic — tiered, trigger-based, designed to be activated before visible disruption peaks — that is directly applicable to organizational workforce planning, not just national policy. The three-tier structure offers a template for scenario-based planning in any institution deploying AI at scale.

**Common misconceptions.** Students and practitioners often assume that AI displacement concerns are either exaggerated hype or a problem for future generations. Anthropic's framework challenges both: it is calibrated to AI capabilities that exist today or will arrive within roughly the next year, and it explicitly flags that critical interventions like pre-distributive capital accounts must begin compounding before disruption is visible. Waiting to act until the problem is obvious is, on this framework's logic, already too late for the most important interventions.

**Suggested framing.** Frame this as contingency planning under uncertainty — a familiar exercise in organizational risk management. You don't know which scenario will materialize, so you design interventions at multiple severity levels and invest now in the foundational systems that make every scenario more manageable. Ask students: what is the organizational equivalent of each tier for a firm deploying AI in its own workforce?
