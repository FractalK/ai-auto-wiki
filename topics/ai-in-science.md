Frontier AI models are increasingly being tested against human scientists on domain-specific research tasks, with results that vary substantially by discipline. As of 2026, AI models outperform human chemists on average on ChemBench, a benchmark covering chemical reasoning and problem-solving. However, the same frontier models score below 20% on astrophysics replication tasks and 33% on Earth observation questions — a pattern the Stanford HAI AI Index 2026 describes as part of the "jagged frontier" of AI capability, where performance is domain-specific rather than uniformly superhuman.

A second pattern emerging across scientific AI is that specialized smaller models frequently outperform much larger general-purpose models on narrow scientific tasks. Scale alone does not predict scientific AI performance: purpose-built models with domain-specific architectures and training sets consistently challenge larger foundation models on their target tasks. Most AI foundation models for science are produced through cross-sector collaborations, in contrast to the industry-dominated landscape of general-purpose AI — reflecting the continued role of academic and government institutions in scientific AI development.
